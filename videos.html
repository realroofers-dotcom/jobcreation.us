The rally tour · 2010

We left with $800, an old van, a gas card, and 2,000 t-shirts nobody would buy. We slept in the van every night, bathed in rivers, and ate the dollar meal twice a day, morning and evening. We never begged. Not one news agency covered any of it, so this is what we filmed ourselves.

2010 · Elkhart, Indiana

The cities that fought the rallies — Elkhart, Indiana

Elkhart was the administration's showcase for the recovery, and the RV industry was the centerpiece of every speech — the president's first trip outside Washington after taking office, four visits, $169 million of Recovery Act money aimed at a county where unemployment hit 20 percent when the plants closed. A rally about job creation turned up, and the city pushed it out of town — to a spot in the middle of a highway, where nobody would see it

What we found on the road · 2010

The tour turned up the thing the Foundation had been arguing for all along — people who could not find work and built their own instead. Some of it was years ahead of what the rest of the country later called a trend.

Nujobi and the news

When no outlet would cover the tour, the answer was not to complain about it — it was to build somewhere the story could live. On the road he also took his first job as a reporter, filing an undercover piece to a New Mexico weekly that traveled far past it. He was paid $50. That gap — between what reporting is worth and what the reporter gets — became Nujobi, and Newsweed.com after it.
